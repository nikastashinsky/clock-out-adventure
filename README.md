# Clock Out

**A chill, silly, hand-drawn platformer about leaving work at 5:00 PM.**

[![Play on itch.io](https://fxwizeguy.itch.io/clock-out-adventure)

> **Play it in your browser (desktop, keyboard):** https://fxwizeguy.itch.io/clock-out-adventure


## The idea

It's 5:00 PM. You are Nik, and all you want is to clock out. The way home is a lot longer than the office door: past the bosses, through a park, down a forest, into a glowing cave, out to a tropical oasis, under the sea, and eventually to a bedtime dream.

It's a gentle game. The jumps are generous, the checkpoints are forgiving, and there's no combat. The challenge shows up as optional secrets, hidden in each level for the curious. Every level has one moment worth stopping for: a whale, an elephant that toots, a cave spider, an alien who says "howdy".

- **13 levels** in two parts, about 30 minutes of play
- **Hand-drawn doodle look**: wobbly pencil lines, paper tones, colour only where nature shows up
- **Everything in one HTML file**: no images, no libraries, no build step
- **All sound is synthesized** with WebAudio, so there are no audio files either
- **Saves** your progress, best times and secrets in your browser

## Controls

| Action | Keys |
|---|---|
| Move | ← → or A D |
| Jump / double jump | Space, ↑ or W (press again in the air) |
| Sprint | Hold Shift |
| Slide | ↓ or S |
| Dash (any of 8 directions) | X, Z, K or J |
| Wall slide / wall jump | Push into a wall, then jump |
| Swim | Direction keys; Space/W up, S down |
| Swing on a lamp or rope | Hold Space |
| Pause / level list | Esc or P |
| Mute | M |

Menus also work with the mouse. The game pauses automatically when you switch tabs.

## Tips

- Sprinting and double jumping get you most places. Try a dash to cross wider gaps.
- Every level hides one secret. They are meant to be hard to reach.
- Coffee gives you a short speed boost.

## How it was made

I built Clock Out with Claude, one prompt at a time, as a way to find out what an AI collaborator could do in a creative workflow. It started with "make a simple stick figure platformer" and ended up as a 13-level game after a few hundred prompts. I never wrote the code myself. I directed it.
